Movement safety

Hard muscular effort is normal; sharp catching, locking or joint pain is not. Stop a movement that causes these symptoms and seek qualified assessment if they persist.

First: set your position

Confirm the saved machine settings, then adjust for comfort and joint alignment. Record a change if the station is different.

Then: control the rep

Lift smoothly, generally lower in 2–3 seconds, and use a range you can control without pain.

Finally: progress

Work about 1–3 reps short of true technical failure. Add weight when both sets reach the top of the range cleanly, not by sacrificing form.

01Chest PressShoulder blades supported; drive handles forward.2 working sets6–12 repsmachine⌄
TARGETChest • front delts • triceps

1. Set up

Arms 1, Seat 4

2. Execute each rep

  1. Set the seat so handles start near mid-chest; sit with feet planted and torso fully supported.
  2. Grip comfortably and bring shoulder blades gently back against the pad without excessive arching.
  3. Exhale as you press forward. Keep wrists stacked over forearms and elbows slightly below shoulder height.
  4. Return slowly until a comfortable chest stretch; keep shoulders from rolling forward.

3. What you should feel

Chest does most of the work, with triceps assisting.

4. Avoid

Bouncing at the bottom, flaring elbows straight sideways, lifting your seat, slamming the stack.

5. Coaching note

If you feel the front of your shoulder rather than chest, check seat height and reduce depth or load.

1. Set up

Select supported lumbar-extension machine or a suitable 45-degree station.

  1. Identify your station first: a lumbar-extension selectorized machine or 45-degree back-extension bench.

Lower-back extensors; 45-degree variation often glutes and hamstrings.

Hyperextending at the top, jerky weighted reps, positioning pad across the abdomen for a hip hinge.

How to use this guide at the gym

  • Tap an exercise to expand all the setup and technique details. The planned rep range is shown on each collapsed card.
  • Rest time: saved values are reference settings from this training template. The other 60–90s values are guide suggestions—not a setting stored in Everyday. Take longer when needed to preserve reps.
  • Two working sets: the four-day template uses two working sets per movement; separate warm-up sets may be needed. Do warm-up sets before heavy compound movements as appropriate.
  • Progression: with 6–12 reps, for example, progress 9/8 → 10/9 → 12/12 before the smallest sensible load increase, then build back up. Compare weights only using the same machine and attachment.
  • Video labels: “University short” is from Nova Southeastern University's exercise demonstration library. Some clips demonstrate a related version, so follow your actual machine's manufacturer instructions first. “Search exact demo” searches YouTube for your equipment variation.
